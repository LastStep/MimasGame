## What is different now

Boons are in the game. In the room you now pray to a god under the preset — Greek, Norse or Hindu, each
naming the Blessing it starts you with — and Ready needs both. Then you play a **best-of-3** instead of a
match: round 1 on Open Field, round 2 on Arena, with `ROUND 2 · 1 – 0` above the turn owner the whole
time. Two seconds after a round ends the board goes dark and three cards drop in — a Blessing, an Enchant
and a Sigil — each with its god, what it does and the item of yours it lands on; click one, press Confirm,
or let the twenty seconds keep the first. A green dot says the other player has already chosen. The series
ends with VICTORY or DEFEAT and `series 2 – 0 · by elimination`, and Back to room. Your boons are in the
examine panel with their god; theirs are `?` rows under `Unknown lineage` until something reveals them, and
then a flyover names the boon and the nameplate names the god. Resigning now concedes the whole series.
Play vs bot is the same series against a bot that prays to a random god and drafts. Nine more boons ship,
three per lineage, plus a new spell — Indra's Storm.

## Deviations from the plan

Five, all small, all forced by the real code rather than chosen:

1. **P8 broke eleven part-1 Core tests, not one.** The spec's §4.1 rewrote what a resign does; it did not
   notice that `SessionTests` used a resign as its cheap way to end a round and reach a draft. Those
   eleven now end the round by elimination. Two existing assertions changed meaning because the rule
   changed under them, which §0.2 could be read to forbid: `Session_RoundEnd_ScoresAndOffersBothPlayers`
   asserts `MatchEndReason.Elimination` where it asserted `Resign`, and `Session_Validate_WrongPhase…`
   asserts a resign in a draft is **accepted** where it asserted `WrongPhase`. Both are the new rule, not
   a softening; `Session_ResignInRoundOne_CountsAsRoundLoss` was replaced by the three resign tests §9 names.
2. **A filtered `DraftStartedEvent` now keeps the hidden side as `null`, not an empty list.** §4.3 wants
   `offers<other>` to be null on the wire, but part 1's constructor normalised null to empty, so the wire
   could not tell "not yours" from "you were offered nothing" (a real case: `rules.draft.offers.loser: 0`).
   The constructor now preserves null, and the two `Assert.Empty` in `Session_Filter_…` became `Assert.Null`
   — strictly stronger. The genuinely-empty case still asserts `Assert.Empty`.
3. **`ServerOptions.MapId` is gone** along with `BotModifierIds`, which §6.5 did name. The session's ladder
   picks every round's map now (D17), so `MapId` decided nothing: a config key that silently does nothing
   is worse than one line of removal. Nothing outside `Room.cs` referenced it.
4. **Round 1 online is `board-3`, not `arena-4`.** Not a choice — it follows from the room hosting a
   session, whose ladder is by `ladderPosition` (board-3 is 1, arena-4 is 3). The spec's §6.6 says
   "`board-3` after `arena-4`", which is the ladder the other way round; the tests assert the real order.
   Worth Rohan knowing, because the first board a player now sees online is the small one, with no props.
5. **No view is sent for the end of a round.** `Session.Match` is null the moment a round ends, so the
   last view a seat gets is the one before the killing blow; the death and the result arrive as events, as
   the presenter already animates them. This is what §3's "`view` is null between rounds" means in
   practice, but it is worth stating: four server tests that asserted `view.IsOver` now assert on the
   `sessionEnded` event and the session block.

## What did not work

**Playing the practice series is what found the real bugs, and it should have come earlier.** Three things
no test could see, all fixed in the last commit (`c813a61`):

- `CanAct` read `Rules.IsOver` without a null check, so **opening the Arena scene threw a
  `NullReferenceException` on the very first frame** — `Rules` is null before round 1 has started, which is
  new. Every other presenter path had been null-checked; this one is a one-line expression and was missed.
  The board click path had the same gap.
- The draft panel sat before the examine panel in the HUD, so its scrim dimmed the board and the turn panel
  and left the action bar and END TURN bright on top of it. It is the last element now.
- The examine panel grew a BOONS section and started running off both ends of the screen on a
  fully-drafted hero. Its body is a `ScrollView` now and the panel is capped at 88 % of the height. This
  was a pre-existing limit — gear + abilities + passives was already tall — that the new section pushed over.

**Three attempts at the draft-overlay screenshot were wasted** before the approach changed. The draft lasts
20 s and opens 2 s in; a Unity MCP `eval` round trip costs several seconds, so "eval to end the round, eval
to check, capture" landed *after* the draft had timed out and the next round had begun — twice, and the
second time it overwrote a good capture with a picture of the wrong thing. The fix is written into STATE:
schedule the screenshot from inside the Editor, in the same eval that ends the round.

**The two-browser best-of-3 was not played.** Spec §7.8 asks for two headed windows playing a whole series
against each other on `localhost`. Driving two browsers through a real round means clicking board tiles to
move and shoot, which a blind coordinate script cannot do reliably — and a scripted version would not be
the thing the spec asked for anyway ("played, not scripted"). What *was* proved in a real browser: the
lobby, the room with its lineage row, picking a god, Ready, and round 1 of an online series with its score
line and lineage tag. What was proved over real sockets: two mirror clients playing a whole best-of-3 with
a draft between rounds, in `SessionTests`. The played series stays where STATE already had it — with Rohan
and a friend, after the deploy.

**Two console errors during the Editor session** were `Failed to handle /api/exec request: Main thread
operation timed out after 5000ms` — the MCP transport giving up on an eval that had in fact run. Not game
errors; everything Mimas itself logged left the console at `consoleErrors: 0`.
