| ADR-023 | 2026-09-16 | **Height-aware line of sight and trajectories.** Sight is a straight ray from the attacker's aim point to the target's, over integer heights in *body units* (`rules.heights`: 1 level = 3 units, hero body 6, aim 4). An intervening column blocks when its top *reaches* the ray — `top·b ≥ h0·(b−a) + h1·a` at a sample `a/b`, a graze blocks — and a column is the tile top plus any living body standing on it. Endpoints and holes never block; unwalkable terrain is solid. An attack declares two independent required fields, `lineOfSight` (must I see you) and `trajectory` (`direct` / `arc` / `sky`, one `ITrajectoryResolver` each in a string-keyed registry), and is legal only if both pass; `apex` is required iff `arc`, and an arc is an integer parabola peaking `max(h0,h1) + apex` sampled at both ends of every intervening tile. Range bands become Euclidean: `minRange² ≤ q²+qr+r² ≤ range²`. | Cover should depend on *where you stand*, not only on what is there, and the two fields let a bow drop an arrow behind a wall it cannot see past while a gun cannot. Keeping everything integer and cross-multiplied (no division, `long` intermediates) keeps server and WebGL client bit-identical, as ADR-013 does for hex lines. Sampling each tile at both of its edges is exact rather than approximate: a tile is a prism and a monotone flight is lowest at an edge. A circular band means a range circle drawn on the board is honest; hex distance would make the drawn circle lie from radius 7 up. Trajectory, apex, sight and the band are read through one seam (`AttackDef.WithTrajectory`), so an Enchant can swap a flight without touching a resolver. Design: `#line-of-sight`, `#trajectories`, `#attacks`. | BattleTech / ASL level buckets (cannot express distance-sensitive cover: a wall blocks the same from 1 tile as from 5); ASL blind-hex tables (the same maths hidden in a lookup table nobody can audit); "an arc ignores everything in between" (nothing left for an apex boon to change, and a wall would never matter to a bow); hex-distance bands drawn as circles (the circle lies); floating-point ballistics (drifts between IL2CPP and the server) |
