# Spec — the camera controls (T-0012, F-camera)

Written 23 Sep 2026 from Rohan's request and his answers the same day. Written for Rohan first: §1–§3 need
no code to read.

## 1. What Rohan asked for, and what he chose

The request, in his words, condensed:

- Q and E rotate the camera counter-clockwise and clockwise.
- W A S D pan the camera.
- Both have a speed that can be tuned in the Inspector.
- Panning must not go too far out of the arena; how far is a tuning value too.
- Space centres the camera back to the default position, the centre of the arena.
- A second camera position, behind the player, looking across at the opponent on the other end — separate
  from today's view, where the two heroes stand left and right.
- The Inspector picks which of the two views a match starts on.

His four answers:

| Question | Chosen |
|---|---|
| How Q/E rotation feels | **Smooth, free**: turns while held, stops where you let go. No snapping to hex faces |
| Online seat 2 sees their hero on the right in today's view | **You always on the left**: the side-on view turns 180° for seat 2 |
| How "behind the player" behaves | **Fixed behind your side**: past your end of the arena, looking across, centred on the arena. It does not follow the hero |
| Extras this session | **Mouse-wheel zoom** and **V flips side-on ↔ behind**. Not chosen: top-down on the same rig, middle-drag pan |

## 2. The controls

| Key | Does | Tuned by |
|---|---|---|
| Q / E (held) | The camera orbits the point it looks at, counter-clockwise / clockwise seen from above | Rotate speed (°/s); an invert switch |
| W A S D (held) | That point slides forward / left / back / right, relative to where the camera faces | Pan speed (units/s); faster zoomed out, slower zoomed in |
| Mouse wheel | Zoom in and out | Zoom speed (units per notch), nearest and furthest distance |
| Space | Glide back to the **default view**: centred on the arena, that view's angle and zoom | Default view (Side On / Behind You) |
| V | Glide to the **other view**, centred | — |
| Tab | Unchanged: swaps to the fixed top-down camera (development) | — |

Every motion glides with one **smoothing** value (0 = instant). The keys only steer while the tilted camera is
the live one, so the top-down camera is untouched and nothing moves behind its back. The wheel does nothing
while the pointer is over the HUD (the examine plate scrolls). The camera keeps working while time is stopped
(the examine preview stops it).

## 3. The two views

The camera orbits a point on the board: a turn (yaw), a tilt (pitch below the horizon) and a distance.

- **Behind You** faces from your spawn towards the opponent's, across the arena's centre.
- **Side On** is Behind You turned a quarter to the left, which always puts your end on the left of the
  screen. For seat 1 on today's maps it is exactly today's camera (turn 0°, tilt 50°, distance 21).

Each view has its own tilt and distance in the Inspector (Side On 50° / 21, Behind You 45° / 21 to start;
tuned from the captures). Your spawn comes from the map by seat, so it is the same every round whatever the
hero has done.

**The pan limit** is a circle round the arena's centre: the outermost tile centres, plus a number of tiles
(Inspector, default 0, may be negative). The Scene view draws it while the rig is selected in Play Mode.

## 4. The code

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `Presentation/Cameras/CameraRigMath.cs` | New. Static and pure: orbit position and rotation, a view's yaw from two spawns, the disc clamp, the pan direction, the smoothing factor, the arena's centre and radius from tile centres. Also `enum ArenaView { SideOn, BehindYou }` |
| `Presentation/Cameras/ArenaCameraRig.cs` | New `MonoBehaviour` on `vcam_Tilted`. Reads the keys (Input System polling, like `CameraDirector`), keeps a target and a current pose, glides one to the other with unscaled time, and writes its own transform. Does nothing until `Frame(board, localSeat)` |
| `Presentation/Match/MatchSession.cs` | One optional field, `_cameraRig`, and one call once the board is built |
| `Presentation/Input/BoardInputController.cs` | `PointerOverOverlay`: whether the HUD claimed the pointer this frame |
| `Tests/EditMode/CameraRigMathTests.cs` | New. The views per seat, the clamp, the extent, the authored pose |

The rig poses the vcam's transform directly (ADR-038): `vcam_Tilted` has no body or aim component, so its
transform already is the shot, and Cinemachine keeps doing the one thing it does here — blending to the
top-down camera on Tab.

## 8. Risks

- **Wheel values differ by platform.** Input System 1.19 normalises scroll to about ±1 per notch; the rig
  clamps a frame's scroll to ±1 so an unnormalised browser value cannot jump the whole range.
- **Rohan's feel.** Speeds and framings are first guesses; they are Inspector values for exactly that reason.
